---
id: case-002
title: Turbocharger failure seven months after supply of a used car on conditional sale
kind: balanced
expected: claimant
---

[1] Mr B (the claimant) complains about Kestrel Motor Finance (the respondent).

[2] On 11 August 2025 Mr B signed a conditional sale agreement with Kestrel Motor Finance for a used estate car, arranged through the dealer Ridgeway Cars.

[3] Mr B collected the car from Ridgeway Cars on 11 August 2025.

[4] When it was supplied, the car was four years old, had 52,300 miles on the clock and had a cash price of £14,200.

[5] Ridgeway Cars' pre-sale invoice shows that, before the sale, it replaced the turbocharger's oil feed pipe with a part not made by the car's manufacturer.

[6] The service history shows that the car was serviced on the day it was supplied and has not been serviced since.

[7] The manufacturer's schedule for this car is a service every 12,500 miles or 12 months, whichever comes first.

[8] On 18 March 2026, at 71,900 miles, the car lost power and the engine warning light came on.

[9] Mr B's local garage, Fenwick Motors, found that the turbocharger had failed.

[10] Fenwick Motors' written report says the replacement oil feed pipe restricted the flow of oil to the turbocharger, and that this caused the failure.

[11] On 2 April 2026 an inspector from Marsh Engineering Assessments, instructed and paid by Kestrel Motor Finance, examined the car.

[12] The inspector's report says the turbocharger failure is consistent with wear at this mileage.

[13] The inspector's report does not mention the replacement oil feed pipe.

[14] Mr B asks Kestrel Motor Finance to pay for a replacement turbocharger, which Fenwick Motors has quoted at £2,350.

[15] Kestrel Motor Finance says the failure was caused by wear and by the missed service, and has refused to pay.
